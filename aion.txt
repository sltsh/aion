Aion comes from the Ancient Greek αἰών (aiṓn). Its core meaning is not simply “eternity,” but more broadly a lifetime, an age, an era, or an extended span of existence/time. In later usage it can also carry the sense of forever / eternity.

What makes it a good name is that it sits between a few ideas at once:
- Time — not clock time, but an age or epoch.
- Continuity — something enduring across long stretches of time.
- Existence — in older Greek usage it could refer to one's life or lifetime.
- Eternity — the later philosophical/religious sense that the word gradually acquired.
It also gave rise to the Latin aevum, meaning an age, lifetime, or eternity; that's ultimately related to English words around “age” and enduring time.
So as a brand, I’d interpret Aion less as literally “eternity” and more as:
an age beyond ordinary time

That works unusually well for a futuristic theme because the word is genuinely ancient, yet Aion looks almost synthetic when written in Latin characters: short, symmetrical-ish, vowel-heavy, no harsh consonants, and the AI opening accidentally gives it a very contemporary technical feel.
There’s also a nice conceptual tension: ancient word, futuristic appearance. That is probably the strongest thing about the name.
